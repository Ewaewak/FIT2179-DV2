Section 01

The Precinct

Within a 1.2km radius of Richmond station, three of Australia's most iconic sporting venues stand side by side. Together they host over 4 million fans per year at Melbourne events alone. This concentration of capacity is unique in the world — and it puts extraordinary pressure on the train lines that serve it.

The Match Day Surge

The difference between a normal Saturday and a match day Saturday is staggering. Jolimont station — with a regular weekend boarding count under 5,000 — surges to over 28,000 boardings when the MCG hosts a blockbuster. Explore how each station responds.

AAMI Park adds a third node to the network on A-League and NRL nights.

Section 04

Does the Network Hold Up?

The COVID-19 pandemic wiped out patronage across the entire metropolitan network. The 2020–2021 collapse was unlike anything since the 1950s. Since then, recovery has been gradual — and by 2024 the key precinct stations are close to — but not yet at — their 2019 peaks. The question is whether the infrastructure designed for pre-COVID demand can now handle post-COVID plus match-day spikes simultaneously.

Annual Patronage Trend 2017–2024 — Key Precinct Stations

Click legend items to highlight individual stations. Hover for annual figures.

Jolimont's 2020 drop was the most severe in proportional terms — losing 67% of boardings in a single year. By 2024 it has recovered to 88% of its pre-pandemic peak, but continues to grow.

The relationship is strongly positive but not perfectly linear — some matches with similar crowds produce different transport patterns depending on the day of week, kick-off time, and which teams are playing (affecting how many fans travel from different parts of the network).
